=== l5-001 | Why we should walk more
focus: linking
Most of us know that we ought to exercise more, but few of us actually do it. Gyms are expensive, and after a long day at work, an hour of running is the last thing anyone wants. I'd like to suggest a simpler answer: walk. Walking costs nothing, needs no special equipment and can easily be added to an ordinary day. Get off the bus a stop early. Take the stairs instead of the lift. Walk to the shops instead of driving. Each of these choices seems small on its own, but together they add up. Doctors say that thirty minutes of brisk walking a day can lower the risk of heart disease and improve our mood. Walking also gives us time to think, to notice our surroundings and to talk to the people we're with. It's an easy habit to start, and an even easier one to keep.

=== l5-003 | In praise of libraries
focus: linking
I've loved libraries ever since I was a child. Every Saturday, my mother took my brother and me to the little library at the end of our road, and we each came home with an armful of books. Today, many people think libraries are out of date. Why borrow a book when you can download one in seconds? But libraries offer much more than books. They are warm, quiet places where anyone can sit for as long as they like without spending a penny. They offer free internet access to people who can't afford it at home, and help to those who are looking for work. They run reading groups for older people and rhyme times for toddlers. In an age when almost everything costs money, the public library is one of the few places left that is open to all of us, and that is worth protecting.

=== l5-004 | The four-day week
focus: rhythm
Should companies move to a four-day working week? I believe they should, and the evidence is on my side. In recent trials, hundreds of firms let their staff work four days instead of five, with no loss of pay. Most of them reported that productivity stayed the same or even rose. Employees took fewer sick days, felt less stressed and were less likely to leave. Of course, this approach won't suit every business. Hospitals and shops need to be staffed every day, and some small firms might struggle to cover the extra hours. But for many office jobs, the long working week is simply a habit, not a necessity. If people can do the same amount of work in less time, why should they spend an extra day at their desks? A shorter week could give us all more time for our families, our friends and ourselves.

=== l5-006 | Learning a language as an adult
focus: sentence-stress
People often say that it's too late to learn a language after the age of thirty. I don't agree. It's true that children seem to pick up new languages without much effort, but adults have advantages of their own. We are better at planning, we understand grammar more easily, and we usually know exactly why we're learning. What adults lack is not ability but time and confidence. We're afraid of making mistakes and of sounding silly in front of others. My advice is to accept those mistakes as part of the process. Speak as often as you can, even if it's only for a few minutes a day. Listen to the radio, watch films with subtitles and talk to yourself in the kitchen. Progress will feel slow at first, but one day you'll realise that you understood a whole conversation, and that's a wonderful feeling.

=== l5-007 | A case for trains
focus: linking
Every summer, millions of us take short flights to cities that we could easily reach by train. I'd like to make the case for the railway. Flying looks quick on paper, but once you add the journey to the airport, the security queues and the wait for your luggage, the train is often just as fast. It's also far more comfortable. On a train, you can walk around, look out of the window and arrive in the centre of a city rather than an hour outside it. And then, of course, there's the environment. A train journey produces a fraction of the emissions of the same trip by air. Rail travel isn't perfect. Tickets can be expensive, and booking across borders can be confusing. But if governments invested in better connections and simpler fares, I think many more of us would leave the plane behind.

=== l5-009 | Screens and children
focus: sentence-stress
How much time should children spend in front of a screen? It's a question that worries almost every parent I know. On one hand, tablets and phones are part of modern life, and children need to learn how to use them. Many apps and programmes are genuinely educational, and video calls let grandparents talk to grandchildren who live far away. On the other hand, too much screen time can affect sleep, reduce physical activity and leave little room for imagination. I don't think strict rules are the answer. Instead, I'd encourage families to agree on simple habits together: no phones at the dinner table, no screens in bedrooms at night and plenty of time outdoors at the weekend. Children copy what they see, so perhaps the most important step is for us adults to put our own phones down a little more often.

=== l5-011 | Against fast fashion
focus: rhythm
The clothes we buy have never been cheaper, and we have never bought so many of them. The average person now buys far more clothing than twenty years ago, and keeps each item for half as long. This is called fast fashion, and it comes at a cost. Producing a single cotton shirt can use thousands of litres of water, and many cheap garments are thrown away after only a few wears. I'm not suggesting that we all stop shopping. But we could ask ourselves a few simple questions before we buy something new. Do I really need it? Will I wear it at least thirty times? Could I find it second-hand? Repairing a favourite jumper, swapping clothes with friends or buying from a charity shop are all small acts, but together they could make a real difference to the planet.

=== l5-013 | Why I volunteer
focus: linking
Every Saturday morning, I spend three hours at a food bank in our town. When I first started, I thought I'd simply be packing boxes and stacking shelves. I do plenty of that, but I've also learned a lot about my own community. The people who come to us aren't who you might expect. Many of them have jobs, some have young families, and most have never needed help before. A broken boiler, an unexpected bill or a reduction in working hours is often all it takes. What surprises me most is how much I get back. I've made good friends among the other volunteers, and I go home every week with a sense of purpose. If you have a few spare hours, I'd really encourage you to find out what's happening near you. Almost every town has an organisation that would welcome an extra pair of hands.

=== l5-014 | Our plan for the high street
focus: rhythm
Thank you for inviting me to speak this evening. Like many towns, ours has seen its high street struggle in recent years. Shops have closed, footfall has fallen and too many windows are empty. But I don't believe the high street is finished. I believe it needs to change. Our plan has three parts. First, we want to turn some empty shops into spaces for small businesses, artists and community groups, with low rents for the first two years. Second, we'll hold a market every Saturday, with local food and crafts. Third, we'll make the street greener and safer, with more trees, more benches and better lighting. None of this will happen overnight, and we'll need your help. But if we work together, I'm confident that our high street can become a place that people want to visit again.

=== l5-015 | Reading for pleasure
focus: sentence-stress
When was the last time you read a book purely for fun? For many adults, the honest answer is years ago. We read constantly, of course: emails, messages, news and endless posts on social media. But reading a novel is a different experience altogether. It asks us to slow down and to stay with one story for hours at a time. Research suggests that people who read fiction regularly are better at understanding how others feel. It also seems to reduce stress and to help us sleep. If you'd like to get back into reading, start small. Choose a book you actually want to read, not one you think you should. Keep it by your bed, and read ten pages each night instead of scrolling on your phone. Before long, you may find that those ten pages have become fifty.

=== l5-017 | Should homework be banned?
focus: rhythm
Every evening, in homes across the country, children and parents argue about homework. Some people now say that homework should be banned altogether, at least in primary schools. I understand their point of view. Young children have already spent six hours at school, and they also need time to play, rest and be with their families. Studies suggest that homework does little to improve the results of younger pupils. But I don't think we should abolish it completely. A small amount of reading each night can build good habits and help parents see what their children are learning. The problem isn't homework itself but too much of it, set without a clear purpose. Instead of a ban, I'd like to see schools agree on short, meaningful tasks, and give children the rest of the evening to simply be children.

=== l5-019 | The value of boredom
focus: linking
Nobody likes being bored. As soon as we have a spare moment, in a queue or on a bus, most of us reach for our phones. But I'd like to argue that boredom is good for us. When our minds have nothing to do, they begin to wander, and that is often when our best ideas arrive. Many writers and inventors say that their most creative thoughts came during long walks or quiet afternoons. Boredom also teaches children to entertain themselves. A child with nothing to do will eventually invent a game, build a den or draw a picture. If we fill every empty minute with entertainment, we never give our imagination the chance to work. So next time you're waiting for a train, try leaving your phone in your pocket. You might be surprised by what comes into your head.
